01 Causes

Six causes behind most fires

The Fire Services Department groups fire cases into a few repeating patterns. Learning to recognise them is the fastest way to reduce risk at home.

Cooking fires

Charcoal and firewood stoves left burning unattended, pots of oil overheating, and open flames close to curtains or stored fuel. Kitchen fire is one of the most frequently recorded categories in Myanmar.

Most common category

Electrical faults

Overloaded sockets, old or damaged wiring, cheap extension leads, and illegal connections. Fire officers repeatedly name faulty wiring alongside negligence as a leading cause in Yangon and Mandalay.

Rising in cities

Candles and oil lamps

During power cuts, candles and oil lamps are often placed on cloth, wood or near bedding. They are knocked over or left burning when the household falls asleep.

Evening risk

Agricultural burning

Slash-and-burn (taungya) clearing, burning crop leftovers and forest burning for hunting. In the dry months these fires escape into forest and reach villages on the forest edge.

Dry season

Arson and conflict

Some fires are started deliberately, including during conflict and communal tension. They are the hardest to prevent, so community reporting and fire watches matter most here.

Hardest to prevent

Cigarettes and hot ash

Discarded cigarette ends, and hot ash or cinders tipped out near fuel, diesel, petrol or engine oil. Small sparks that smoulder for hours before anyone notices.

Entirely preventable

More figures worth knowing

  • 63% of all fire cases happen in five regions — Yangon, Mandalay, Ayeyarwady, Sagaing and Bago OCHA / Logistics Cluster, Myanmar
  • 132 fires in Mandalay Region, January to October 2025 MFSD
  • 101 fires in Shan State, January to October 2025 MFSD
  • 2,644 houses damaged by fire, January to October 2025, plus 13 factories and 38 warehouses MFSD
  • 7 months of dry weather each year, when fire risk is highest UN-Habitat Myanmar fire manual
Note: these statistics are approximate and are based on available reports from the Myanmar Fire Services Department, OCHA Myanmar and UN-Habitat. Different sources count fires in different ways (for example, whether crop or forest fires are included), so totals will not always match exactly. Always check the latest official figures before quoting a number in your own project.
03 Preparedness

What you can do, step by step

Almost every item here costs nothing. These are the habits the Myanmar Fire Services Department asks families to follow all year, and especially from March to May.

01

Home fire safety

  • Keep a bucket of water, sand and a fire extinguisher near the kitchen.
  • Never leave cooking, candles or oil lamps burning when you leave the room or sleep.
  • Keep exits, stairs and doorways clear — no stored sacks or boxes blocking the way out.
  • Keep matches and lighters out of reach of children.
  • Check smoke alarms monthly if your home has them, and test the battery.
02

Electrical safety

  • Do not plug many appliances into one socket or daisy-chain extension leads.
  • Replace cracked plugs, frayed cords and old wiring — ask a qualified electrician.
  • Keep cables away from cooking heat and never run them under mats or bedding.
  • Switch off and unplug appliances before a storm or a long absence.
  • Never use water on an electrical fire — switch off the power first.
03

Cooking safety

  • Place stoves on a stable, non-flammable surface, away from curtains and bamboo walls.
  • Turn pot handles inward so they cannot be knocked over.
  • Put out charcoal completely with water and stir the ash before disposing of it.
  • Never pour water into hot oil — cover the pan and turn off the heat.
  • Store fuel, kerosene and gas cylinders well away from the stove.
04

If a fire starts

  • Raise the alarm — shout, knock on doors, warn your neighbours.
  • Call 191 and give your address and nearest landmark clearly.
  • Get out by the nearest safe exit; crawl low under smoke.
  • Never go back inside for belongings or animals — wait for firefighters.
  • If your clothes catch fire: stop, drop to the ground and roll to smother the flames.

Emergency numbers in Myanmar

191 Fire and rescue
192 Ambulance
199 Police
01-295-133 Myanmar Red Cross

Save these numbers in every family member's phone today, and agree on one meeting place outside your home.

Why it works

Fire officers in Myanmar repeat one message: most fires start from a small, ordinary mistake that nobody noticed in time. Clearing an exit, checking a plug or turning off a stove takes seconds. Rebuilding a house takes years.
